Welcome to the proceedings of the 1st Workshop on Grounding Language Models:
Learning Faithfully and Efficiently (GroundLM 2026), held in conjunction with
EMNLP 2026. GroundLM brings together researchers and practitioners interested
in the principles, methods, benchmarks, and applications that help language
models use evidence faithfully and communicate the basis of their outputs.

The workshop addresses grounding in a broad sense, including retrieval-
augmented generation, multimodal and visual evidence, scientific and technical
reasoning, tool use, attribution, uncertainty, and the evaluation of factuality
and reliability. A central goal is to connect advances in model capability with
auditable behavior: systems should not only produce useful answers, but should
also be able to identify, use, and justify the evidence on which those answers
depend.

We received 147 submissions across the GroundLM tracks. The regular main-paper
track accepted 56 papers, corresponding to an acceptance rate of approximately
38.1\%. These proceedings also contain 24 Findings papers and 10 shared-task
system reports, which represent additional contributions selected through their
respective evaluation processes. Together, the papers in this volume reflect a
wide range of perspectives, from new datasets and benchmarks to modeling
techniques, empirical studies, and practical systems for grounded language
understanding and generation.

The program is complemented by invited talks from Freda Shi (University of
Waterloo and Vector Institute), Lei Li (Carnegie Mellon University), Krzysztof
Czarnecki (University of Waterloo), Valentino Maiorca (Institute of Science and
Technology Austria), Danae Sánchez (University of Copenhagen), Anya Belz
(Dublin City University), Iryna Gurevych (Technical University of Darmstadt),
Ivan Titov (University of Edinburgh), and Sina Zarrieß (University of

challenges in grounding: Sina Zarrieß (University of Bielefeld), Carolin
Lawrence (NEC Laboratories Europe), Amin Shabani (RBC Borealis), Dirk Hovy
(Bocconi University), and Krisztian Balog (University of Stavanger and Google
DeepMind). Their participation makes the workshop a place not only to present
results, but also to exchange ideas across the language, vision, information
retrieval, and scientific machine learning communities.
